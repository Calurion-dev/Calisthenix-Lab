Level 02 · Static skills

Hold, without moving.

Three skills, three levels. Static holds are not grabbed: they are built with prerequisites, progressions and patience.

A static skill is a held position, motionless, where the body resists gravity without momentum. It demands strength, but above all control: duration never replaces alignment.

The three guides below follow the same principle: you only move to the next step when the previous one is clean and comfortable. Prerequisites are markers, not exams; progressions are directions, not races.

A prerequisite is not a permissionThe numbers give a direction, not a green light. If the position degrades, the set is over — even if there are seconds left on the clock.

The guides

Three skills, three paths.

01

L-sit

Intermediate

Hold your legs horizontal, hips lifted, without touching the floor.

Support
Parallettes or floor
Level
Intermediate
Target
3 × 5–15 s

Prerequisites

  • 30 s of clean hollow body hold
  • 20 s support hold, shoulders down
  • 8 to 10 controlled leg raises

Muscles

Abs, hip flexors and quads; triceps and shoulders in support.

Frequency

3 to 4 sessions per week, 10 to 15 minutes, before strength work. Stop before the position degrades.

Progressions

  1. Foot-supported hold. 3 × 15–20 s, hips lifted.
  2. Tuck L-sit. 3 × 10–15 s, knees to chest.
  3. One leg extended. 3 × 8–12 s per side.
  4. Full L-sit. 3 × 5–15 s, legs horizontal.

Move on when you can hold 3 × 15 s clean, without shrugging.

Common mistakes

  • Shrugged shoulders or rounded back.
  • Legs dropping below horizontal.
  • Holding your breath.

Safety. Wrists and shoulders work hard: warm them up, use parallettes if the floor is uncomfortable, and stop if you feel pain.

02

Planche

Advanced

Body horizontal above the hands, arms straight.

Support
Floor or parallettes
Level
Advanced
Target
Tuck 3 × 10–15 s

Prerequisites

  • 30 s of clean hollow body hold
  • 20 s planche lean, shoulders over hands
  • 30 s support hold
  • 8 to 10 controlled dips

Muscles

Shoulders (anterior deltoid), chest, triceps, serratus, abs and wrists.

Frequency

2 to 3 sessions per week, 10 to 20 minutes, when fresh. 48 h between sessions; deload every 4 to 6 weeks.

Progressions

  1. Planche lean. 3 × 15–20 s, shoulders in front of the wrists.
  2. Tuck planche. 3 × 10–15 s, knees to chest.
  3. Advanced tuck. 3 × 5–10 s, hips open.
  4. Straddle. 3 × 3–8 s, legs apart.

Open the hips when you hold 3 × 20 s of lean and 3 × 15 s of tuck clean.

Common mistakes

  • Bent arms.
  • Piked or raised hips.
  • Shrugged shoulders.
  • Collapsing wrists.

Safety. The planche loads wrists and shoulders heavily. Progress slowly, never to failure, and stop if pain. The full planche is a matter of years, not weeks.

03

Front lever

Advanced

Body horizontal under the bar, arms straight.

Support
High bar
Level
Advanced
Target
Tuck 3 × 10–15 s

Prerequisites

  • 8 to 10 strict pull-ups
  • 30 s of clean hollow body hold
  • 20 s active hang, shoulders down
  • 10 controlled leg raises

Muscles

Lats, abs, glutes, scapular muscles and biceps isometrically.

Frequency

2 to 3 sessions per week, 10 to 20 minutes, when fresh. 48 h between sessions.

Progressions

  1. Tuck front lever. 3 × 10–15 s, knees to chest.
  2. Advanced tuck. 3 × 8–12 s, hips open.
  3. One leg extended. 3 × 5–10 s per side.
  4. Straddle or full. 3 × 3–8 s, body horizontal.

Extend the legs when you hold 3 × 15 s of tuck with hips level.

Common mistakes

  • Bent hips.
  • Piked body.
  • Shrugged shoulders.
  • Bent arms.

Safety. Check the bar and your grip. Warm up shoulders and elbows; stop if pain. The full version is advanced.
