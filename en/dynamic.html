Level 03 · Coming

Add momentum.

The muscle-up, the handstand and transitions are not pure strength moves. They are timing, control and coordination — after the fundamentals.

Static, then dynamic

A dynamic skill adds movement, sometimes momentum, to a position you can already hold. The muscle-up, for instance, is not “a stronger pull-up”: it is a transition between a pull and a support. Without a solid support and a clean pull, there is nothing to connect.

That is why order matters. The fundamentals build strength and stability. Static skills teach you to hold a position under tension. Dynamic skills teach you to move from one position to another without losing control.

Three examples

  • Muscle-up — move from a straight-arm hang to a support above the bar in one continuous motion. It demands solid pull-ups and a stable support.
  • Handstand — balance on your hands with your body aligned. Bracing and shoulder position matter more than arm strength.
  • Transitions — link several positions, on a bar or on the floor, while staying in control. This is where the basic work really shows.

Why wait

Dynamic skills look spectacular, and that is exactly what pushes people to skip steps. The problem is not the movement itself: it is what it asks of your shoulders, elbows and wrists when the stabilising muscles cannot keep up. A forced muscle-up with unstable shoulders is an injury that can cost months.

Progression is not a soft precaution. It is what makes skills possible and durable. When you get there, each skill will have its guide: measured prerequisites, steps, mistakes and passing criteria.

Simple ruleIf you cannot hold the final position without momentum, you cannot reach it with momentum yet. Work on the position, not the skill.
