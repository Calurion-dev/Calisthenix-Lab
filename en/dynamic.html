Level 03 · Dynamic skills

Move, without losing control.

Three skills, three levels. Dynamic work is not forced: it is built with prerequisites, progressions and patience.

A dynamic skill adds movement to a position you can already hold. The muscle-up links a pull to a support; the handstand moves your balance onto your hands; transitions link two positions without rest.

The three guides below follow the same principle as the static skills: move on only when the previous step is clean and comfortable. The prerequisites are markers, not exams — and the clock never replaces control.

A prerequisite is not a permissionThe numbers below indicate a starting level, not a right to try. If the position degrades, the set is over — even if there are reps left on the counter.

The guides

Three skills, three paths.

01

Muscle-up

Advanced

Move from a hang to a support above the bar, without momentum.

Support
High bar
Level
Advanced
Target
3 × 1–3 reps

Prerequisites

  • 8–10 strict pull-ups, no momentum
  • 10–15 clean dips
  • 30 s hollow body hold
  • 20 s support hold above the bar

Muscles

Lats, pectorals, triceps, deltoids and core.

Frequency

2–3 sessions per week, fresh, 48 h between sessions. Quality, not failure.

Progressions

  1. High pull-up. 3 × 3–5, chest to the bar.
  2. Negative transition. 3 × 3–5, from support to hang, slowly.
  3. Assisted muscle-up. 3 × 3–5 with a band or one foot.
  4. Strict muscle-up. 3 × 1–3, no momentum.

Move on when the transition stays controlled and the elbows pass above the bar without a jolt.

Common mistakes

  • Kipping or leg drive.
  • One elbow then the other (chicken wing).
  • Pull too low, chest far from the bar.
  • Shoulders collapsing in support.

Safety. Solid bar, clear space, warm shoulders and wrists. Never force a muscle-up alone on a doubtful bar; stop if you feel shoulder or elbow pain.

02

Handstand

Intermediate

Hold your balance on your hands, body aligned, without the wall.

Support
Floor or wall
Level
Intermediate
Target
3 × 10–20 s

Prerequisites

  • 30 s plank hold
  • 20 s wall handstand, back to the wall
  • 10 clean push-ups
  • Warm, mobile wrists

Muscles

Shoulders, triceps, core, glutes and wrists.

Frequency

3–4 sessions per week, 10–15 minutes, before fatigue. The wall is a tool, not a failure.

Progressions

  1. Back to the wall. 3 × 20–30 s, body aligned.
  2. Stomach to the wall + pushes. 3 × 5–8, heels off.
  3. Free balance, starting at the wall. 3 × 3–5 attempts of 5–10 s.
  4. Free handstand. 3 × 10–20 s.

Move on when you can hold 30 s at the wall without arching and correct your balance with your fingers.

Common mistakes

  • Arched back (banana).
  • Bent arms.
  • Looking at your feet.
  • Passive fingers, no correction.

Safety. Clear wall, mat, warm wrists. Do not train free balance alone if you cannot exit the position; learn to roll or step down.

03

Transitions

Advanced

Link two static positions on the floor, without rest, while staying in control.

Support
Parallettes or floor
Level
Advanced
Target
3 × 2–3 transitions

Prerequisites

  • 15 s tuck L-sit
  • 15 s tuck planche
  • 20 s support hold, shoulders down
  • 10 clean dips

Muscles

Shoulders, triceps, pectorals, core and hip flexors.

Frequency

2–3 sessions per week, 10–15 minutes, fresh. 48 h between sessions.

Progressions

  1. Weight shift. 3 × 5–8, from support forward, shoulders over hands.
  2. Tuck L-sit → tuck planche. 3 × 3–5, controlled descent.
  3. Tuck planche → tuck L-sit. 3 × 3–5, controlled return.
  4. Full sequence. 3 × 2–3, without putting your feet down.

This guide works transitions on the floor, between two static positions. Bar transitions come next.

Move on when each position holds for 2 s and the transition does not break alignment.

Common mistakes

  • Positions not mastered separately.
  • Bent arms.
  • Hips dropping.
  • Speed replacing control.

Safety. Wrists and shoulders are heavily loaded. Stable parallettes, full warm-up, never to failure. If the position degrades, put your feet down.
