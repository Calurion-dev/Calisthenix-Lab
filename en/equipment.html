Equipment · Cross-cutting level

Two purchases are enough to start.

A pull-up bar and dip bars. Everything else is a bonus — useful only if it meets a specific need.

Equipment does not make the practitioner, but it can make progress easier. This guide follows a simple ranking: what is essential, what genuinely helps, and what can wait.

The three levels below are not rigid boxes. A beginner can buy rings if they have the space and budget; an advanced practitioner can skip parallettes if they do not train the planche. The badges show the marker for each stage.

The essentialsA pull-up bar and dip bars. With these two purchases, you can already build a complete practice. Everything else is a bonus.

Pull-up bar

Essential

Buying

In what order?

  1. The pull-up bar. The best first purchase: it opens up pull-ups and much of the bar work.
  2. The dip bars. They complement the bar and allow dips, support holds and street workout skills.
  3. Rings and chalk, if the budget allows. Rings are versatile; chalk improves grip.
  4. Resistance bands if you lack strength, then a dipping belt once reps become easy.
  5. The weighted vest, grip and parallettes only for a specific goal: load, sweat, planche.

SafetyCheck that the bar and supports are stable before every session. Equipment does not replace a warm-up or technique. If you feel pain, stop and consult a healthcare professional.
