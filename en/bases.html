Fundamentals · Core of the site

Six movements that carry everything else.

Push, pull, bend your legs, brace your trunk. These are the six guides to work through before chasing a skill. Each one covers execution, breathing, mistakes, an easier version and a harder one.

The six movements below are not a warm-up before the “real” skills. They are the real skills. A clean push-up, a controlled row, a full squat: this is the foundation that muscle-ups, handstands and everything else are built on.

Why insist? Because your body adapts to what you ask of it, not to what you watch. A poorly executed movement repeated a hundred times installs a bad habit and loads the joints in the wrong place. A clean movement, even with few reps, builds useful strength: stable shoulders, a solid back, active bracing.

You don't need sophisticated equipment. A floor, a low bar or a solid table, two stable supports: that's enough to start. Choose the version that lets you do 6 to 12 clean reps, without jerking. If you can't feel the movement, stop the set: quality before quantity.

01

Push-up

Push

The most accessible pushing movement: it builds chest, triceps and shoulders while asking your trunk to stay braced.

Focus
Horizontal push
Equipment
Floor
Level
Beginner

How to perform it

  1. Place your hands slightly wider than your shoulders, fingers pointing forward.
  2. Lower your chest toward the floor keeping your body aligned, elbows at about 45°.
  3. Push into the floor until your arms are straight, without letting your lower back sag.

Breathe. Inhale as you lower, exhale as you push.

Common mistakes

  • Arched lower back or hips in the air.
  • Elbows flared out at 90°.
  • Cut range: the chest stays far from the floor.

EasierKnee push-ups, or hands elevated on a table.

HarderFeet elevated, then archer push-ups.

Safety. No shoulder or wrist pain: reduce the range or elevate your hands.

02

Australian row

Pull

The horizontal pull: it balances pushing work and strengthens the back, which beginners often neglect.

Focus
Horizontal pull
Equipment
Low bar or table
Level
Beginner

How to perform it

  1. Set a bar at hip height, or lie under a solid table.
  2. Hang with straight arms, body braced, heels on the floor.
  3. Pull your chest toward the bar, squeezing your shoulder blades together, then lower slowly.

Breathe. Exhale as you pull, inhale as you lower.

Common mistakes

  • Hips dropping toward the floor.
  • Shoulders creeping up to your ears.
  • Lowering too fast, without control.

EasierHigher bar, bent knees, or a band-assisted row.

HarderLower bar, feet elevated, then pull-ups.

Safety. The bar or table must hold your weight without moving. Check the fixings before pulling.

03

Dips

Push

The vertical push: shoulders, triceps and chest, with solid bracing so you don't collapse at the bottom.

Focus
Vertical push
Equipment
Two stable supports
Level
Intermediate

How to perform it

  1. Place your hands on two solid supports, arms straight, shoulders down.
  2. Lower by bending your elbows to about 90°, torso slightly forward.
  3. Push through your palms to rise, without shrugging your shoulders.

Breathe. Inhale as you lower, exhale as you rise.

Common mistakes

  • Going too low and feeling the shoulder collapse.
  • Shoulders rising toward the ears.
  • Torso too upright, loading the front of the shoulder.

EasierBench dips with feet on the floor, or close-grip push-ups.

HarderParallel bar dips, then weighted dips.

Safety. A sharp pain at the front of the shoulder? Reduce the range before pushing further.

04

Squat

Legs

The fundamental leg movement: thighs, glutes, ankles and bracing, with no added load.

Focus
Legs
Equipment
Floor
Level
Beginner

How to perform it

  1. Feet hip-width apart, toes slightly turned out.
  2. Sit down by pushing your hips back, knees tracking over your feet.
  3. Stand by pushing into the floor without lifting your heels.

Breathe. Inhale as you lower, exhale as you stand.

Common mistakes

  • Knees caving inward.
  • Heels lifting at the bottom.
  • Lower back rounding at the end of the descent.

EasierBox squat onto a chair, or a controlled partial squat.

HarderBulgarian split squat, then assisted pistol squat.

Safety. Persistent knee discomfort: reduce the range and consult a professional.

05

Forward lunge

Legs

Unilateral work: each leg carries its own weight, which exposes imbalances and stabilises the hips.

Focus
Legs, balance
Equipment
Floor
Level
Beginner

How to perform it

  1. Take a long step forward, feet in line.
  2. Lower until both knees are at 90°, the back knee just above the floor.
  3. Push through the front foot to return, then switch legs.

Breathe. Inhale as you lower, exhale as you push.

Common mistakes

  • Front knee collapsing inward.
  • Torso tipping forward.
  • Step too short, blocking the descent.

EasierSplit squat in place with a hand on a wall or chair.

HarderWalking lunge, then jumping lunge.

Safety. Keep a support within reach if your balance is shaky.

06

Plank

Core

The foundation of everything else: it teaches your trunk to transfer force between your arms and legs.

Focus
Bracing
Equipment
Floor
Level
Beginner

How to perform it

  1. Place your forearms on the floor, elbows under your shoulders.
  2. Align your head, hips and heels.
  3. Squeeze your glutes and brace your abs without holding your breath.

Breathe. Breathe normally, through your nose if possible.

Common mistakes

  • Hips sagging toward the floor.
  • Hips too high, back in a V.
  • Head dropping or lifting.

EasierKnee plank, or a high plank with straight arms.

HarderSingle-leg plank, then side plank.

Safety. If your lower back aches, stop the set: your brace has given out.

How many sets?

Vulgarisation stops where a personalised programme begins. These are general guidelines: choose a difficulty that leaves two reps in reserve, and increase the number before making the movement harder.

Leave 48 hours between two sessions that target the same muscle groups. Discomfort that lasts more than a few days deserves a healthcare professional's opinion.

2–3 sets 6–12 reps 20–40 s plank 60–90 s rest 2–3 sessions / week
