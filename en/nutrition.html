Nutrition · Cross-cutting level

One plate, three zones.

Half for vegetables and fruit, a quarter for carbohydrates, a quarter for the rest. A visual guide to build a meal — not a prescription.

  • 50 %Vegetables & fruit
  • 25 %Carbohydrates
  • 25 %The rest
    protein & fats

The plate is not a diet. It is a visual starting point: it avoids counting every calorie and reminds you of the proportions of a complete meal. Exact amounts depend on your weight, activity and goals — the plate gives structure, not gram-level precision.

50 % — Vegetables & fruit

They bring volume, fibre, vitamins and minerals. Raw or cooked, fresh or frozen: half the plate at every main meal. Vary colours and cooking methods.

25 % — Carbohydrates

Rice, pasta, potatoes, bread, oats, legumes. The portion adapts to load: larger on training days, smaller on rest days.

25 % — The rest

Protein (eggs, fish, meat, tofu, legumes) and fats (oil, nuts, avocado). A protein source at every meal, a little fat for taste and satiety.

In practice

Three markers.

  • One plate = one main meal. No need to weigh: proportions are enough for most meals.
  • Carbs are the dial. Increase the portion around training, reduce it if you move little.
  • Water as the default drink. No supplement replaces a complete plate.
